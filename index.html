Posterior Refinement:
Fast Language Generation via Any-Order Flow Maps

TL;DR

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) decide how confidently to commit each token a priori — from noise and context alone, before the rest of the sequence exists — and their independently factorized posteriors cannot recover from errors that only become visible once a full draft is written. Flow Map Language Models fix this factorization error by transporting the whole sequence jointly, but give up the any-order flexibility of MDMs. We introduce FMLM+, which equips flow maps with masking-style noise schedules, and Posterior Refinement, an inference strategy that drafts a complete sequence in a single forward pass, scores each token a posteriori against the full draft, then re-noises and regenerates only the low-confidence tokens. This lets the model self-correct in parallel, matching or surpassing the strongest baselines with up to 32× fewer function evaluations.

Abstract

Non-autoregressive generation offers a powerful paradigm for iterative refinement, allowing models to recursively critique, erase and regenerate arbitrary subsets of tokens. However, existing non-autoregressive models fail to realize this potential. Masked Diffusion Models (MDMs) suffer from factorization error, causing sample quality to collapse when generating multiple tokens simultaneously. Flow Map Language Models (FMLMs) circumvent this bottleneck via joint sequence transport for excellent few-step generation, but sacrifice the inference-time flexibility of MDMs.

We introduce FMLM+, a framework that bridges this gap by equipping FMLM with masking-style noise schedules. While generating the full sequence in a single step, FMLM+ simultaneously scores the global consistency of each token a posteriori. We leverage this to introduce Posterior Refinement, a novel inference-time refinement strategy that enables the model to adaptively self-correct its outputs, matching the performance of discrete baselines with 32× fewer NFEs. Across diverse benchmarks, we demonstrate that FMLM+ with Posterior Refinement improves the speed–quality tradeoff over both MDM and FMLM families, providing a scalable foundation for high-fidelity language modeling.

How Posterior Refinement Works

Posterior Refinement lets FMLM+ judge the fit of each token after the fact and fix its own mistakes in parallel. Starting from a single-pass draft, the model decodes every position and scores each token's posterior confidence given the entire draft. It commits only the high-confidence tokens (correct, high confidence) and re-noises the rest (correct but low confidence, incorrect — both low confidence) for the next round, now conditioned on the tokens just committed. Crucially, the model's incorrect tokens consistently fall within its low-confidence set, so refinement reliably filters and revises errors. Over a handful of rounds, the committed region grows until the puzzle is solved.

A-Priori vs. A-Posteriori Confidence

Both the MDM denoiser and the FMLM+ single-step denoiser δ0,0 predict only token-wise marginal posteriors. To decide which tokens to keep, they estimate confidence a priori — from noise and context alone, before a full sequence exists. This conflates the aleatoric uncertainty of the data with the model's epistemic confidence: a token that looks confident in isolation may be globally inconsistent, and because these marginals factorize independently, the model cannot recover from such an error without re-sampling from scratch.

Key idea. Because FMLM+ transports the entire sequence jointly under a shared flow, the one-step map δ0,1 exposes a well-defined posterior confidence for every token given the full draft — exactly the signal a factorized MDM cannot provide. Posterior Refinement turns this signal into a self-correction loop.

This difference is decisive on multi-modal targets. On unconditional Sudoku, a-priori confidence — from both MDLM and FMLM+'s δ0,0 — collapses at low NFE, while FMLM+'s a-posteriori δ0,1 reaches 87% accuracy in just a few evaluations.

FMLM+: Token-wise Noise Schedules

FMLM+ generalizes Flow Map Language Models by lifting the single global diffusion time to a per-token time vector t = (t1, …, tL). Pinning tl = 1 freezes a position at its clean value to act as conditioning context, while tl < 1 leaves it noised. Implemented as a per-position time in the DiT's adaptive layer norm, this needs no architectural changes for conditioning: it inherits the few-step efficiency of FMLM and the any-order inference flexibility of MDMs, recovering parallel, block-diffusion, and confidence-based sampling as special cases.

The Posterior Refinement Loop

Each round drafts a full sequence (one, or a few, function evaluations), keeps the tokens whose posterior probability pmax exceeds a threshold p, and re-noises the rest for the next round. To avoid stalling when no position clears the bar, at least k positions are committed by top-k confidence. Lower p commits faster (fewer rounds, lower quality); higher p is more conservative and converges to higher quality.

Posterior Refinement

Posterior Refinement delivers large efficiency gains across all four benchmarks. On Sudoku, FMLM+ reaches 97.9 / 92.0 / 71.2 (Easy/Med./Hard) at just 4 NFEs — surpassing every baseline evaluated at 128–1024 NFEs. On GSM8K, it reaches 19.0% zero-shot accuracy at 32 NFEs, a 32× speedup over the strongest non-autoregressive baselines at matched accuracy. On TinyStories and OpenWebText, FMLM+ matches or surpasses every diffusion baseline with up to 8× fewer evaluations.

Connection to Masked Diffusion

The masked diffusion objective is exactly the boundary case s = t = 0 of the FMLM+ training objective. This correspondence makes the growing pool of pretrained MDMs directly usable to accelerate FMLM+ training, via either distillation (using the MDM as a teacher) or direct warm-starting from its weights. Warm-starting, FMLM+ (Init), performs best across every NFE budget — with its largest margins in the few-step regime, evidence that FMLM+ is less susceptible than MDLM to the factorization error of parallel decoding.
